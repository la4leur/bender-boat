# M/V HATCHLING Demo Walkthrough
## Walking Eric Bardot Through the Bender Boat Solution
### Prepared for Jim Andrews — Ops Normal AI LLC

---

> **Setup:** Have the HATCHLING demo open in a browser. Start on the Vessel tab. Keep this script on a second screen or printed. Estimated walk-through time: 20–25 minutes.

---

## OPENING (30 seconds)

> "Eric, what I'm going to show you isn't a mockup or a slideshow. This is a working prototype built specifically around your fleet — three SANSU Bender Class vessels, your crew structure, and the exact workflows you described in your requirements. The test vessel is called HATCHLING, and every feature you're about to see maps directly to what you told us you need."

---

## TAB 1: VESSEL (2 minutes)

**Click the Vessel tab if not already there.**

> "This is your vessel dashboard — the at-a-glance view for HATCHLING. Up top you've got the basics: vessel type, IMO number, flag state, classification. Below that, the active voyage — Norfolk to Bermuda, 680 nautical miles, currently underway."

**Point to the position roster.**

> "Here's your crew roster organized by department — Deck and Engine. Every position shows who's in the seat, their credential status, and when their current rotation ends. Green means certs are current and valid. You'd see yellow or red if anything was expiring."

**Point to the compliance stats.**

> "These four numbers — crew count, average sea days this rotation, STCW compliance, and credential status — that's your regulatory snapshot. If an auditor walks aboard or you need to pull a report for the office, it's right here. No digging through spreadsheets."

**Transition:**
> "Let me show you who these people are."

---

## TAB 2: CREW (2 minutes)

**Click the Crew tab.**

> "Fourteen mariners in the system — nine currently aboard HATCHLING, five in the relief pool. Every card gives you the key info at a glance: position, department, credential count and status, sea days this rotation, and their rotation end date."

**Click to expand one crew card — suggest Captain Mike Sullivan.**

> "Click into anyone and you get the full picture — license details, STCW certificates, contact info, emergency contact, and their complete rotation schedule. This is the kind of stuff that right now probably lives across three or four different spreadsheets and someone's email."

**Point to credential status indicators.**

> "See how credentials show green with the expiry date? The system tracks every cert. When something is coming up for renewal, this goes yellow at 90 days and red at 30. You'll never have someone show up for a rotation with an expired TWIC again."

**Transition:**
> "Now let's look at how a voyage actually works."

---

## TAB 3: VOYAGE (2 minutes)

**Click the Voyage tab.**

> "This is your active voyage — Norfolk to Bermuda. You've got departure and arrival times, total distance, and a live progress bar. Below that, the waypoint timeline shows every planned position along the route."

**Point to the crew manifest section.**

> "Here's something important — the voyage doesn't just track the ship, it tracks everyone on it. Full manifest with positions and watch assignments. When this voyage closes, every crew member's sea time is automatically calculated and logged. No manual entries, no forgetting someone."

**Point to the sea time preview.**

> "See this? The system already knows that when HATCHLING arrives in Bermuda, Mike Sullivan will have accumulated 5 days and 8 hours of sea time on this leg. That number flows into their total sea time record, their credential tracking, everything downstream."

**Transition:**
> "Let's look at how the watch system works."

---

## TAB 4: WATCH (2 minutes)

**Click the Watch tab.**

> "Three watches running 24 hours — 4 on, 8 off. The active watch is highlighted. You can see exactly who's on deck and who's on engineering watch right now."

**Point to the watch rotation visual.**

> "Each watch shows the OOW — Officer of the Watch — and the rest of the watch team. The offgoing watch officer signs the log for their watch. That's standard practice, and it's built into the system. No ambiguity about who's responsible for what happened on whose watch."

**Point to the work hours compliance table.**

> "This table is your STCW compliance view. Every crew member's hours worked, hours rested, and whether they're within STCW limits. Right now everyone's green. If someone were approaching limits — say they got called out during their off-watch for an emergency — the system flags it immediately. That's not just good practice, that's your liability protection."

**Transition:**
> "OK, now we're getting to the part that I think is going to change how you think about crew operations. Let me show you a crew change."

---

## TAB 5: CREW CHANGE ⭐ (5 minutes)

**Click the Crew Change tab. Take a beat — this is the centerpiece.**

> "This is an interactive crew change scenario. I'm going to walk you through exactly what happens when you need to swap out your Chief Mate — from booking travel to handling the inevitable curveball. Every step you see here is how the actual system will work."

### Step 1: Book Travel
**The scenario loads showing Lisa Chen as Chief Mate relief, flying SFO to Norfolk.**

> "Lisa Chen is your incoming Chief Mate relief. The system already knows her home airport, her credentials, and when she needs to be aboard. It's pulled flight options — and notice these aren't just regular consumer fares. You see the marine fare option? Those are the discounted rates through C Teleport that are specifically for crew travel. Right now you're paying ATPI about $2,000 a month for this kind of service."

**Click to book a flight.**

> "One click. Flight's booked, cost is logged, and it's tied to Lisa, to this voyage, and to this crew change event."

### Step 2: Voyage Delay
**Click through to the delay scenario.**

> "Now here's real life. Weather pushes your arrival back two days. Watch what happens — the system automatically flags Lisa's flight. She's now arriving before the vessel, which means hotel costs, or she needs to rebook. The system caught this the moment the voyage plan updated."

### Step 3: Rebook
**Click through the rebooking.**

> "New flight options appear automatically. Look at the cost — the original fare, the new fare, the delta. If there's a change fee, it's captured. If the marine fare policy covers it, that's noted too. One click to rebook, and the cost event chain is maintained. Kelly can see exactly what happened and why."

### Step 4: Cancellation
**Click through the cancellation.**

> "Lisa has a medical issue and can't make the rotation. Now you've got a cancelled booking, a refund to track, and you need a replacement — all at once. In the current workflow, that's probably three phone calls, four emails, and Kelly trying to figure out the refund status two weeks later. Here, it's all captured in one place."

### Step 5: Replacement
**Click through the replacement flow.**

> "This is where the system really earns its keep. It scans your relief pool, checks credentials, checks availability, and suggests the best option. It's recommending Tom Halverson to promote to acting Chief Mate and bringing in Andy Brooks as Third Mate relief. Look — it verified their credentials automatically. Tom has the right license, the right STCW certs, everything checks out."

**Point to the audit trail at the bottom.**

> "And here's the full audit trail. Every action, every decision, timestamped. If anyone ever asks 'why did we book that flight' or 'who approved the replacement' — it's all here."

**Transition:**
> "Now, let me show you what Kelly sees."

---

## TAB 6: CHANGES (3 minutes)

**Click the Changes tab.**

> "This is Kelly's world. Every dollar that moves through crew travel flows through here — bookings, changes, refunds, cancellations. Not in a spreadsheet, not in email confirmations she has to dig through. Right here, organized and filterable."

**Point to the summary cards.**

> "Six numbers across the top — total booked, changes, cancellations, refunds pending, refunds credited, and net spend. Kelly can see at a glance where the money is."

**Click through the filter options.**

> "She can filter by time period, by event type, and by dollar amount. Looking for all changes over $500? Two clicks. Need to see everything on the current voyage? One click."

**Click the "By Crew" view.**

> "This is the view that replaces the spreadsheet reconciliation. Every cost event grouped by crew member. Lisa Chen — booked $487, cancelled, refund pending $487, net zero. Andy Brooks — replacement booking, $342. Kelly can see the full financial story of every crew member's travel."

**Click the "Refunds" view.**

> "And here's the refund tracker. Pending, processing, credited — with amounts. No more calling the airline to check on a refund status. No more wondering if that $487 ever came back."

**Transition:**
> "Now let me show you what we built for the travel planning side — this is what replaces ATPI."

---

## TAB 7: TRAVEL (5 minutes)

**Click the Travel tab. You'll see four sub-tabs.**

### Sub-tab: Search & Compare

> "Remember, you're paying ATPI about $2,000 a month — $24,000 a year — for crew travel management. Here's what replaces that."

**Show the three crew search cards.**

> "Three crew members need to travel for this crew change. Each one has fuzzy search criteria — not just one airport, but multiple origin airports near their home. Lisa's near San Francisco, so we're searching SFO, Oakland, and San Jose. Not just Norfolk as a destination — we're also checking Newport News and Richmond. And not just one date — a window of June 15th through 18th."

**Click into Lisa's search results.**

> "Side-by-side comparison. Every option shows the full cost breakdown — flight, hotel if they're arriving the night before, ground transport from the airport to the vessel. The total trip cost, not just the airfare. That's a number ATPI doesn't give you."

**Point to the tags.**

> "See the tags? Best value, fastest, most flexible, direct flights, marine fares. You can filter by any of these. Want to see only refundable fares? One click. Only direct flights within budget? Two clicks."

**Select one option per crew member to show the summary table.**

> "Select an option for each crew member and you get the total crew change travel cost at the bottom. That's the number you put in front of Kelly for budget approval before a single ticket is booked."

### Sub-tab: Ground Logistics

**Click the Ground Logistics sub-tab.**

> "This is something ATPI definitely doesn't do. Once flights are booked, the system builds a ground logistics plan. Who's arriving when, who's staying where, who's got a rental car, who's riding with whom, and what order you pick everyone up."

**Point to the pickup sequence.**

> "Dave Winters arrives the evening before — he's the priority arrival for the captain handover briefing. He's got the rental car. Next day, Lisa arrives at 1340 at Norfolk, Andy arrives at 0730 at Newport News — Dave picks up Andy on the way to the dock since PHF is en route. It even accounts for the fact that Lisa needs 10 hours of rest before she can stand watch because she's crossing three time zones."

**Point to the logistics tags.**

> "Every crew member has tags — traveling together, first-time arrival needs extra guidance, rental car driver, heavy gear for the engineer. This is the kind of coordination that right now happens over text messages and phone calls. Here, it's a plan everyone can see."

### Sub-tab: Travel Packets

**Click the Travel Packets sub-tab.**

> "Once the plan is set, one button pushes a travel packet to every crew member — their flight details, hotel confirmation, pickup instructions, vessel berth location, port agent contact, emergency numbers. Push via SMS, email, or both."

**Click Preview on one of the packets.**

> "Here's what Lisa gets. Everything she needs to get from her front door to the vessel, in one message. When plans change — and they always change — hit the update button and crew gets the revised packet with changes highlighted. No more forwarding email chains."

> "And crew can pull their own packet from a portal link — no login needed, just a secure token URL. You can push the same plan to everyone traveling together, or customize per person."

### Sub-tab: Visa Tracker

**Click the Visa Tracker sub-tab.**

> "You're running blue-water boats that hit international ports. This tracks every visa requirement for every crew member for every destination."

**Point to the dashboard numbers.**

> "Four requests in the system right now for the Bermuda crew change. Two are straightforward — US passport holders don't need a Bermuda crew visa. But look at this one — your Korean crew member's visa is processing with 22 days until the crew change. And this one — your Colombian crew member's visa is flagged critical because the processing time is longer than the time remaining."

**Point to the document checklist.**

> "Each request has a document checklist — passport copy, seaman's book, letter of invitation, crew visa application, photos. The system tracks what's been gathered, what's missing, and what's been submitted. No more wondering if someone remembered to send the documents to the port agent."

**Point to the urgency calculation.**

> "See this? The system calculates urgency automatically — days until crew change minus average processing time equals your submit-by deadline. If you're inside that window, it goes red. That's the kind of thing that falls through the cracks when you're managing it manually, and it can strand a crew member in the wrong country."

## HANDLING COMMON QUESTIONS

**"What about our other two vessels?"**
> "Same instance, same database. You add a vessel in the system and it inherits the same crew pool, same watch structure, same travel workflow. Your three SANSU Bender Class boats share crew already — the system is designed for that."

**"What does this cost?"**
> "We'll give you a firm fixed price for the build. No recurring subscription fees, no per-seat licensing. You're paying for the development and you own what we build. Hosting costs are minimal — we're talking about $20 to $50 a month for the server, not $44,000 a year."

**"Can Kelly actually use this?"**
> "Kelly's view is the Changes tab and the Travel Planner. She doesn't need to see the watch system or the navigation log. Role-based access means she sees exactly what she needs — expenses, refunds, travel bookings — and nothing she doesn't."

**"How does this compare to HELM's features?"**
> "HELM gives you planned maintenance, some crew management, and basic voyage tracking. What they don't give you is the travel integration, the expense tracking, the visa management, or the ground logistics. You'd still need ATPI for travel and spreadsheets for the rest. We're replacing both in one system."

**"What about maintenance tracking?"**
> "That's on the post-V1 roadmap. We focused first on the crew and travel side because that's where your pain is and where the money is. Planned maintenance, parts inventory, and work orders are Phase 2. And because you own the codebase, you can prioritize what comes next."

**"What if we need changes after delivery?"**
> "You own the source code. We can make changes, you can hire someone else to make changes, or you can modify it yourself. No vendor lock-in. That's the whole point."
